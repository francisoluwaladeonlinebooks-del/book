# Evidence audit: Bio-Harmonization

Working date: 19 November 2026. Scope: the uploaded 303-page print proof (a 153-sheet spread layout) and its 16 chapters, epilogue, four appendices and reference list.

Status key: **Verified** = checked against a primary guideline, government or public-health source. **Reframed** = a real underlying idea kept, with the unsupported number or mechanism removed. **Removed** = no defensible source found; the claim does not appear in the revised manuscript. **Unverifiable** = depends on private records that were not supplied.

## A. Claims that triggered the remediation

| # | Original claim and location | Finding | Action in the revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "89% improved sleep within 4 weeks", "78% reduced pain and inflammation", "67% achieved hormonal balance within 6 months", "84% felt heard" (Author's Note, ch. 7) | Unverifiable. No protocol, sample, ethics approval, questionnaire or dataset was provided. "Hormonal balance" is undefined. | Removed. No outcome percentages appear in the revised book. |
| 2 | "Original data from 50,000+ women", "300+ peer-reviewed studies", pilots across 12 or 32 countries (Author's Note, ch. 16, bio) | Unverifiable. No dataset, report, register or named institution was supplied. | Removed. The book no longer claims original research. |
| 3 | Global Women's Bureau described as a research organisation with teams in 32 countries, six continents, named specialists and a 2025 founding (copyright page, Author's Note, bio) | Unverifiable and presented as established fact. | Reframed. | 4 | "Peer reviewers and medical editors scrutinised the claims" | No reviewers named; no sign-off supplied. | Removed. A blank independent clinical review packet is provided in `editorial/clinical-review-packet.md`; the book does not claim review until a licensed reviewer signs. |
| 5 | "One in ten women has PCOS" (ch. 2) | Verified as a reasonable rounded statement. WHO currently states an estimated 10 to 13% of reproductive-age women; prevalence varies with diagnostic criteria. | Kept with a WHO citation and a note that estimates vary. [1] |
| 6 | "PCOS reversal through cycle syncing" (ch. 8 title and story) | Removed as a treatment claim. Systematic reviews do not support phase-based training or nutrition as a treatment for PCOS; no reversal data were supplied. | Chapter retitled "The hormonal harmony protocol". PCOS section follows the 2023 international guideline and states that PCOS is a long-term condition. [2] |
| 7 | "Women need 30% more protein after 40" (ch. 10) | Not supported. Adult RDA is 0.8 g/kg/day; expert groups suggest 1.0 to 1.2 g/kg/day for healthy adults over 65, with individual variation. | Reframed as a weight-based discussion with RDA and older-adult guidance, plus a kidney-disease caveat. | 8 | "67% of burnt-out women have undiagnosed hormonal dysfunction", "42% subclinical hypothyroidism at TSH >2.5", "71% HPA rhythm disruption" (ch. 3) | Unverifiable; the TSH threshold is presented as disease without a clinical context. | Removed. | 9 | "Women need 20% more sleep than men" (ch. 3, ch. 5) | Not supported by guideline bodies. AASM and CDC give 7+ hours for adults, with no separate female target. | Reframed: adults need at least 7 hours; women report more disturbed sleep. [5] |
| 10 | Shift work harms women "40% faster", "33% more menstrual irregularities", "35% more cardiovascular disease" (ch. 5) | Specific comparative figures not verified. | 11 | "68% of women menstruated during the new moon"; lunar light exposure triggers ovulation (ch. 5) | Not supported by reliable human evidence. | Removed. The chapter keeps light and sleep routines and calls lunar synchrony an unproven idea. |
| 12 | "4.2x more likely to receive an accurate diagnosis" from 90-day tracking, "60% faster diagnosis", "43% higher treatment success" (ch. 6) | Unverifiable. | Removed. The dossier chapter explains that tracking can improve the quality of the conversation; it makes no outcome guarantee. |
| 13 | Morning light "increases serotonin 42%", protein breakfast "reduces blood sugar variability 34%", light curfew "increases melatonin 58%", protocol "reduces sleep medication 61%" (ch. 7) | Unverifiable mechanistic percentages. | Removed. | 14 | "Women with PCOS see 67% cycle regulation within 6 months of cycle syncing", "testosterone dropped 68 to 32 ng/dL", "metabolism increases 5 to 10% in luteal phase" (ch. 8) | Unverifiable and inconsistent with review evidence on cycle phase. | Removed. | 15 | "4-7-8 breathing reduces cortisol 16%", "somatic exercises reduce pain 47%", "61% show nervous system dysregulation" (ch. 9) | Unverifiable. | 16 | "Intermittent fasting worsens HPA function in 43% of stressed women", "65% of women over 40 insulin resistant" (ch. 10) | Unverifiable. | Removed. Fasting is described as optional, with clear groups who should avoid it and no frequency claims. [8] |

## B. Sources retained in the revised reference list

All retained sources are public and checkable. Full citation text is in `manuscript/references.md`.

1. WHO, Polycystic ovary syndrome fact sheet; 10 to 13% estimate and diagnostic caveats.
2. Teede HJ et al. (2023), international evidence-based PCOS guideline, *Fertility and Sterility* 120(4):767-793, doi:10.1016/j.fertnstert.2023.07.025.
3. IOM (2005) protein RDA; Bauer J et al. (2013) PROT-AGE; Deutz NEP et al. (2014) ESPEN.
4. Cadegiani FA & Kater CE (2016), *BMC Endocrine Disorders*, systematic review on "adrenal fatigue".
5. AASM/SRS (2015) sleep consensus; CDC sleep guidance.
6. IARC Monographs Vol. 124 (2019/2020), night shift work, Group 2A.
7. Systematic reviews on menstrual cycle phase and training/performance (Elliott-Sale et al. 2021; Colenso-Semple et al.; Niering et al. 2024).
8. eClinicalMedicine (2024) umbrella review of intermittent fasting trials; clinical cautions from eating-disorder and obstetric guidance.
9. NIH/NIEHS and peer-reviewed reviews on autoimmune disease sex distribution; standard thyroid-disease guidance from ATA/Endocrine Society patient information.
10. Endocrine Society (2024) vitamin D clinical practice guideline; NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheet.
11. WEF (2025) article citing 5% of global health R&D funding; used only as a sourced estimate.
12. Chen CC et al. (2008), *Academic Emergency Medicine*, 981 patients, abdominal pain.
13. Guzikevits M et al. (2024), *PNAS* 121(33):e2401331121, doi:10.1073/pnas.2401331121.
14. WEF & McKinsey Health Institute (2024), *Closing the Women's Health Gap*, modelled $1 trillion annual GDP opportunity by 2040.
15. WHO (2020) physical activity guidelines; WHO endometriosis fact sheet; NAMS (2022) hormone therapy position statement; ACOG/peer-reviewed app-accuracy literature; Clayton & Collins (2014) and Beery & Zucker (2011) on sex bias in research.
