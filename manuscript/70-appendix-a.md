# Appendix A: The 90-day record

This is a record-keeping schedule, not a treatment plan. Each block adds one thing to observe. If a habit is unsuitable for you, leave it out. If symptoms worsen, stop testing and speak to a clinician.

## Weeks 1 to 4: baseline

- Write the date, cycle day or calendar date, sleep hours and wake-ups.
- Note one symptom score from 1 to 10 and one sentence about the day.
- Go outside within an hour of waking on as many days as you can.
- Keep a consistent wake time where your schedule allows.

At the end of week 4, write what changed and what did not.

## Weeks 5 to 8: food and movement notes

- Add one food note: whether you ate regular meals and where energy dipped.
- Add one movement note: what you did and how you felt two hours later.
- If you want to try more protein at breakfast, choose a source you enjoy and can afford.
- If you have a history of disordered eating, skip food targets and keep the dates only.

At the end of week 8, mark the days that were unusual. Look for what happened the day before.

## Weeks 9 to 12: evening and review

- Lower the lights in the last hour before bed, using a version that fits your household.
- Keep the sleep and symptom notes going.
- Read the full twelve weeks. Write down three patterns you can see and one you cannot explain.
- Take the one-page summary from chapter 6 to an appointment if a pattern concerns you.

## A sample day

- On waking: note the time, sleep hours and any night symptoms.
- Before leaving the house: ten minutes outside if possible.
- Midday: three-minute break away from the screen.
- Evening: note pain, mood and temperature.
- Bedtime: lights down, phone away from the face.

## A simple shopping list

This list is optional. It contains ordinary foods, not supplements.

Eggs, plain yoghurt, beans or lentils, tofu, tinned fish, oats, rice, frozen vegetables, fruit, olive oil, wholegrain bread. Choose what you can access and what fits your culture and budget.

## Meal templates

- Breakfast: a protein source plus a familiar carbohydrate and fruit.
- Lunch: beans, eggs, fish or tofu with vegetables and a staple.
- Dinner: what you would normally cook, with attention to whether you ate enough.

## Movement progression

- Start with a ten-minute walk most days if that is feasible.
- Add two short strength sessions a week: sit-to-stands, wall push-ups and carrying practice.
- Increase one variable at a time, no more than once a fortnight.
- Rest when a flare, injury or illness calls for it.

## What to do at day 90

Keep the notebook. The value of a record grows when it covers a year, not a fortnight.
