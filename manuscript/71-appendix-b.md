# Appendix B: Tests and results

This appendix explains common tests in general terms. Reference intervals differ between laboratories, and reference intervals are not the same as treatment thresholds. A clinician interprets a result with your history, symptoms, medicines and pregnancy status. Do not order tests from this list without a clinical reason, and do not change treatment on the basis of a number alone.

## Tests a clinician may consider

- Full blood count: anaemia and infection markers. Ferritin is a measure of iron stores and rises during inflammation.
- Thyroid function: TSH is usually the first test; free T4 may be added. TPO antibodies support a diagnosis of autoimmune thyroiditis but do not by themselves set treatment.
- Glucose and HbA1c: HbA1c reflects average glucose over roughly three months. Anaemia and some haemoglobins can affect the result.
- Lipid panel: total cholesterol, HDL, LDL and triglycerides, interpreted with overall cardiovascular risk.
- Vitamin D: 25(OH)D. Testing is not recommended routinely in healthy adults [1]; specific medical indications exist.
- B12 and folate: considered when diet, absorption or symptoms suggest deficiency.
- Inflammation: hsCRP or ESR, used alongside symptoms rather than as a stand-alone score.
- Hormones: oestradiol, FSH, LH, testosterone, SHBG and progesterone may be requested for specific questions. Timing and the assay matter. A single result in perimenopause can be misleading.
- PCOS assessment: clinical signs, ultrasound when indicated, and tests to exclude thyroid disease, high prolactin and other causes [2].
- Endometriosis: clinical assessment and imaging; a definitive diagnosis sometimes requires surgery [3].
- Autoimmune conditions: ANA and specific antibody tests are ordered when symptoms and examination point in that direction. A positive ANA alone does not diagnose disease.

## Common thresholds used in adult care

These are examples from widely used guidelines, not universal rules and not targets for self-treatment.

- HbA1c below 5.7% is usually described as normal; 5.7 to 6.4% indicates increased risk; 6.5% or above on two suitable tests meets a common diagnostic threshold for diabetes [4].
- Fasting plasma glucose below 100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L) is usual; 100 to 125 mg/dL indicates impaired fasting glucose; 126 mg/dL or above meets a diagnostic threshold when confirmed.
- TSH reference intervals often sit near 0.4 to 4.0 mIU/L, with variation by laboratory and population. Pregnancy has different ranges.
- 25(OH)D: the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements describes 20 ng/mL (50 nmol/L) or more as generally adequate for bone health for most people; levels above 50 ng/mL have been linked to possible harm [5]. The 2024 Endocrine Society guideline advises against routine testing in healthy adults [1].
- Vitamin B12: many laboratories use roughly 200 pg/mL as the lower end of their reference interval; symptoms and other tests matter.
- Ferritin: a low result can indicate depleted iron stores; inflammation can raise it. Thresholds differ by condition and country.

## Tests promoted directly to consumers

Be cautious with panels sold without a clinical question. These include broad food-sensitivity IgG panels, staged salivary cortisol curves sold as a diagnosis of "adrenal fatigue", comprehensive stool panels marketed for general wellness, and genetic panels sold for vague metabolic problems. Results from these tests often lead to unnecessary restriction and anxiety. Ask what a result would change before paying for it.

## Result flags that need prompt attention

Contact a clinician promptly about a result labelled critical by the laboratory, very high or very low thyroid results, a fasting glucose in the diabetic range, severe anaemia, or any result accompanied by chest pain, fainting, confusion or severe weakness. Emergency symptoms do not wait for a test.

## Retesting

Retesting is usually most useful when it answers a specific question. A clinician may repeat a test to confirm an abnormal result, to monitor treatment, or when symptoms change. Repeating the same panel every month without a question tends to create noise.
