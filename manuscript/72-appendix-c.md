# Appendix C: Where to look next

Links and services change. Treat this list as a starting point and check the organisation's current page. The book has no official partnership with any of them.

## Public-health guidance

- World Health Organization: who.int. Search for the fact sheets on PCOS, endometriosis and physical activity.
- Centers for Disease Control and Prevention: cdc.gov. Sleep guidance and general women's health information.
- MedlinePlus: medlineplus.gov. Plain-language articles from the US National Library of Medicine.
- NHS: nhs.uk. Condition guides and guidance on when to seek urgent care.

## Professional bodies

- The Menopause Society: menopause.org. Clinician directory and position statements.
- American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists: acog.org. Patient FAQs and practice guidance.
- Endocrine Society: endocrine.org. Guidelines on thyroid, vitamin D and adrenal conditions.
- International pelvic pain and pelvic-floor therapy organisations can be found through national physical-therapy registries.

## Research

- PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov. Search with a specific question and read the abstract before the headline.
- Cochrane Library: cochrane.org. Summaries of systematic reviews.
- WHO Global Alliance for Women's Health information is published through weforum.org.

## Finding a clinician

Use your national medical regulator's register to confirm that a clinician is licensed. In Nigeria, check the Medical and Dental Council of Nigeria register. Elsewhere, use the equivalent national or state regulator. Professional society directories are useful for specialists, but a directory listing is not a guarantee of a good fit.

Ask before booking: what the first appointment includes, whether a referral is needed, how results are communicated, and what the follow-up arrangement is.

## Mental-health support

For urgent risk, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line in your country. For ongoing concerns, use a licensed mental-health professional. Trauma-informed approaches can be helpful, but the credential and the fit matter more than the label.

## Tracking tools

A paper notebook works. If you use an app, check whether it is intended as a wellness tool or a regulated medical device. Do not rely on a consumer cycle app to prevent pregnancy. Read the privacy policy and turn off sharing you do not need.

## Questions to ask any resource

- Who wrote this, and what are their qualifications?
- What evidence is cited, and can I see the source?
- Does the page sell a product?
- What would change my mind if the claim were wrong?
