# Chapter 11: Inflammation and autoimmune disease

Keisha was diagnosed with Hashimoto's thyroiditis at 34. Claims that most cases can be put into remission through diet and supplement plans have no reliable trial behind them.

Keisha started levothyroxine, had her thyroid function checked repeatedly until the dose was right, learned that some days would still be difficult, and made ordinary changes to sleep and activity. She also stopped paying for a supplement plan that her endocrinologist could not reconcile with her labs.

## The facts worth keeping

Autoimmune diseases are a group of more than 80 conditions in which the immune system attacks the body's own tissues. Estimates of their combined prevalence vary, and a commonly cited public-health figure puts them at roughly 8% of the population in the United States. About 80% of people diagnosed with autoimmune disease are women [1]. The reasons involve genetics, sex hormones, X-chromosome biology and environmental exposures; the subject is active research, not settled fact.

Hashimoto's thyroiditis is an autoimmune condition of the thyroid. Standard treatment is thyroid hormone replacement with monitoring. Someone can feel better and have normal thyroid results while still having days affected by sleep, mood or other conditions. Antibody numbers alone are not a treatment target for everyone.

The word "remission" needs care. In some autoimmune diseases it has a formal definition; in others it is used loosely. A person feeling well is good news. It is a different thing from a cure, and neither justifies stopping prescribed medication without medical advice.

## Diet claims that outran the evidence

The gut and the immune system are connected, and coeliac disease is a real indication for a gluten-free diet. Neither fact makes restrictive eating a general treatment for autoimmune disease. There is no fixed percentage of the immune system that lives in the gut.

There is a real connection between the gut and the immune system. There is also a real condition, coeliac disease, in which gluten causes harm and a strict gluten-free diet is treatment. A gluten-free diet has not been shown to treat Hashimoto's in people without coeliac disease. Testing for coeliac disease before starting a restrictive diet matters, because the test becomes less reliable once gluten is removed.

Vitamin D illustrates why "optimal" needs a source. The 2024 Endocrine Society guideline advises against routine 25(OH)D testing in healthy adults and no longer endorses a single target level to define sufficiency [2]. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements treats 20 ng/mL or more as generally adequate for bone health in most people, with higher levels linked to possible harm [3]. People with specific diagnoses follow their clinician's advice.

Food sensitivity panels that measure IgG antibodies are not a reliable way to identify foods that cause symptoms. An elimination diet supervised by a clinician or dietitian has a place when a specific condition is suspected; a long list of forbidden foods bought from an advertisement does not.

## Environmental exposures

Endocrine-disrupting chemicals are a legitimate research topic. Some are found in plastics, pesticides and consumer products, and exposure varies widely by occupation, location and product use. The difficult part is attributing an individual disease to an individual exposure.

Practical steps are modest and low cost: ventilate when using solvents or strong cleaners, follow instructions on pesticides, store food in glass or stainless steel where practical, avoid heating food in plastic, and wash produce. There is no kitchen audit that will remove every chemical and no supplement that will "detox" the body. The liver and kidneys do that work.

## Sleep, movement and flares

Autoimmune symptoms fluctuate. A record with dates can help separate a flare from a bad week. Note sleep, menstrual phase if relevant, medication changes, infections and stress. Bring it to appointments.

Regular moderate movement supports general health. During a flare, the right amount may be smaller. Physiotherapy and occupational therapy can help when pain or fatigue is limiting function.

Vaccination decisions depend on the diagnosis and the treatment, especially when the immune system is suppressed. Ask the treating clinician rather than a general online checklist.

## Questions to ask a clinician

- Which of my results are we treating, and what target are we aiming for?
- Does my pattern fit one autoimmune condition, or could more than one be present?
- Should I be tested for coeliac disease before changing my diet?
- Are there interactions between my supplements and my medicines?

## The standing record

For two weeks, note energy, pain, sleep and any new medication or supplement on the same page. Mark days that were clearly worse. Look for a change in timing before looking for a cause.
