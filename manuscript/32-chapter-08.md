# Chapter 8: The hormonal harmony routine

Priya was diagnosed with PCOS at 28 after two years of trying to conceive. In the story she tracks cycles, asks for a metabolic work-up and follows a plan developed with a clinician.

PCOS is a long-term condition. The 2023 international guideline describes diagnosis using a set of criteria that include infrequent ovulation, signs of excess androgen and polycystic ovaries on ultrasound, after other conditions have been excluded [1]. Management depends on what a person wants: cycle regularity, symptom control, metabolic health or fertility.

## What cycle tracking can do

A cycle is a series of events, not a personality. Follicular phase, ovulation and luteal phase describe what the ovary and uterus are doing. Energy, mood and appetite vary between people and between cycles. A chart can help you notice your own pattern.

For a regular cycle, a simple journal is enough: date, bleeding, cervical mucus if you choose to observe it, basal temperature if it interests you, sleep and symptoms. Basal temperature confirms that ovulation has already happened; it does not predict a fertile window reliably on its own [2].

For an irregular cycle, the same record is more useful because it shows how irregular the cycle is. Take the chart to an appointment. Do not treat the chart as a diagnosis.

## The cycle-syncing question

Cycle syncing means changing training, food or work to match an assumed hormonal phase. It is popular, and the claim is often made that it regulates hormones or improves performance.

The evidence is weaker than the marketing. Reviews of exercise studies find that menstrual phase has little or no consistent effect on strength or endurance performance, and that many studies fail to verify the phase they claim to measure [3]. Reviews of nutrition and phase-based eating are small and inconsistent. No trial has shown that cycle syncing reverses PCOS.

You can still use a cycle chart to plan rest. If your own record shows that cramps and poor sleep cluster in certain days, arranging lighter work then is sensible. The reason is your observed pattern, not a rule printed on a chart.

## PCOS: what the guideline supports

The 2023 guideline recommends lifestyle support for all women with PCOS, including healthy eating and regular physical activity. It recommends metformin for some adults with a higher body mass index and metabolic features, combined oral contraceptives for menstrual irregularity and hyperandrogenism when pregnancy is not the goal, and letrozole as first-line treatment for ovulation induction when pregnancy is the goal [1]. These are clinical decisions with risks and contraindications. They belong in a consultation.

Ask about blood pressure, glucose and lipids as part of assessment. Ask about mood and sleep, since the guideline also addresses screening for depression and sleep apnoea. A supplement sold for "hormone balance" has not been shown to replace any of this.

## Perimenopause and irregular cycles

In perimenopause, ovulation becomes inconsistent and cycle length changes. A phase chart built on a textbook 28-day cycle will be misleading. Track bleeding dates, night sweats, sleep and mood, and bring the dates. If you are over 45 and periods have stopped for 12 months, that is the usual clinical definition of menopause; before that, pregnancy is still possible [4].

Hormone therapy remains the most effective treatment for bothersome hot flushes and night sweats for many people, with benefits and risks that depend on age, time since menopause, dose, route and personal history. The 2022 position statement from the Menopause Society describes a more favourable balance for healthy women under 60 or within ten years of menopause onset, and a less favourable balance when started later or after 60 [5]. Compounded "bioidentical" products carry safety concerns. This is a decision to make with a prescriber.

## The routine, rewritten

The chapter keeps a four-part routine because it gives readers something to do:

1. Track dates and symptoms for three cycles.
2. Note which days are genuinely difficult and what helped.
3. Ask for a review if cycles are absent, very irregular, or changing after a long period of regularity.
4. Bring the record to the appointment and ask which treatments fit your goals.

## The standing record

Write the date your last period started, the usual interval between periods, and one sentence about what you want from treatment. Bring those three lines to your next appointment.
